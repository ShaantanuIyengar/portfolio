Clinical ECG Signal Processing & FIR Filter Design

Characterisation and noise attenuation of real-world electrocardiogram signals using custom FIR band-pass filters in MATLAB.

1. Project Background & Time Series Characterisation

Refined and characterised a real-world clinical electrocardiogram (ECG) from the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database. Source data from PhysioNet was resampled from 360 Hz to 128 Hz for digital analysis, establishing a spectral baseline to design a custom FIR filter.

2. Frequency Spectrum Analysis

A spectrum analysis of the 10-second ECG recording shows:

  • Dominant Cardiac Peak: Labeled at 1.20 Hz (approx. 72 BPM) with an amplitude of 0.0187 mV.
  • Band Limit Proof: Spectral energy is concentrated below 20 Hz, confirming that a 0.5 to 40 Hz filter will preserve cardiac waves while rejecting interference.

3. Filter Specification & Hamming Window Design

Ambulatory ECG signals are corrupted by two main sources of real-world interference:

  • Baseline Wander (<0.5 Hz): Slow low-frequency drift from respiration and movement that shifts the baseline and obscures critical ST-segments.
  • High-Frequency Noise (>40 Hz): Distortion from muscle activity and instrumentation that masks QRS complex details.

We designed a 115-tap FIR band-pass filter (0.5 to 40 Hz) using a Hamming window to achieve the target 46 dB stop-band attenuation, cleaning both noise sources simultaneously.

4. Pre- and Post-Filtering Comparison

Applying the filter resolves both types of interference:

  • Waveform Refinement: Slowly shifting drift and high-frequency ripples are attenuated while keeping QRS complex heights stable.
  • Spectral Filtering: Attenuates out-of-band noise by 46.02 dB while retaining key pass-band cardiac energy, losing no diagnostic content.

5. Outcomes

The MATLAB implementation successfully isolates clinical cardiac features. A zero-phase filter implementation ensures no temporal shift of critical peak alignments, preserving diagnostic accuracy.
