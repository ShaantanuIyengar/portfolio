BLDC Motor Drive System & EV Powertrain Simulation

An end-to-end EV powertrain co-simulation coupling LTspice inverter circuit power stage with ANSYS Maxwell 2D transient electromagnetic FEA.

1. End-to-End System Context & Industrial Relevance

This project demonstrates a complete end-to-end EV powertrain motor drive simulation, integrating the electrical drive stage in LTspice with the electromagnetic and mechanical motor output stage in ANSYS Maxwell 2D.

  • Electrical Drive Synthesis (LTspice): Models the 3-phase inverter power stage and high-frequency PWM switching logic responsible for converting DC bus power into variable-frequency AC currents.
  • Electromagnetic FEA (ANSYS Maxwell 2D): Models transient flux density, spatial saturation, phase currents, and electromagnetic torque output of the motor rotor and stator assembly.
  • Industry Utility: Similar virtual prototyping workflows are used by power electronics and electric vehicle engineers at companies such as Tesla, BorgWarner, and Dyson to validate inverter switching and motor torque output prior to building costly hardware.
PART 1 • ELECTRICAL DRIVE STAGE

2. Three-Phase Inverter Circuit Simulation (LTspice)

The electrical drive stage is a three-phase DC-AC PWM inverter constructed with six STN3N45K3 MOSFETs, converting a 314V DC bus into variable-frequency AC power.

Circuit & Waveform Analysis

  • Phase Voltage PWM (Cause): MOSFETs switch between high and low states, generating high-frequency square waves.
  • Synthesised Phase Current (Effect): LC filtering smooths pulses into 120-degree phase-shifted sinusoidal currents of approximately 15A, proving balanced three-phase power delivery.
  • Solver & Stability: Utilised the SPICE Alternate Solver with Gear integration to resolve high-voltage switching transients and overcome non-ideal component convergence issues.

PART 2 • ELECTROMAGNETIC & MECHANICAL STAGE

3. Computational Electromagnetic FEA (ANSYS Maxwell 2D)

The mechanical output stage uses an ANSYS Maxwell 2D transient electromagnetic solver to simulate magnetic flux density distribution, phase currents, and output torque of the BLDC motor.

GOVERNING VECTOR POTENTIAL DIFFUSION EQUATION
∇ × ( (1/μ) ∇ × A ) + σ(∂A / ∂t) = J   |   B = ∇ × A

Where A is magnetic vector potential, μ is magnetic permeability, σ is electrical conductivity, and J is current density. A vector potential boundary condition of A = 0 Wb/m constrains the magnetic field within the solution domain.

SOLVE SETUP Transient setup with stop time 0.2s and fixed time step 0.002s (100 intervals) at 1552.95 rpm.
SOLUTION TYPE Transient magnetic with cylindrical-about-Z geometry to compute dynamic rotor movement.
MESH REFINEMENT Surface deviation of 0.037mm and normal deviation of 15° across magnets and air-gap.
EXCITATIONS & GEOMETRY External stranded windings for phases A, B, C; XG196 permanent magnets on cyan rotor.

4. Critical Reflection & Model Limitations

Circuit Model Evaluation

Successfully synthesised a stable high-voltage inverter stage and resolved SPICE solver convergence. Future builds could incorporate dead-time hardware logic to eliminate shoot-through risks, as well as parasitic PCB trace inductances.

FEA Model Evaluation

Captured spatial flux saturation and torque ripple that circuit solvers miss. Being 2D, it neglects 3D end-winding leakage effects and assumes ideal sinusoidal drive currents rather than real PWM switching harmonics.
