Synchronous Buck Converter - Circuit and PCB Design

12V → 5V @ 5A • 300kHz Switching • 4-Layer KiCad Design

SECTION 01 • OVERVIEW

A synchronous buck converter PCB designed in KiCad to convert 12V to 5V at 5A continuous current, switching at 300kHz.

  • Two-Iteration Build: Developed as an initial 2-layer design built to standard manufacturing guidelines, and a revised 4-layer design incorporating thermal and signal integrity optimisations.
  • DFM & Datasheet Validation: Every design decision — trace widths, via geometry, layer stackup, component placement — is justified against manufacturer capabilities and component datasheets.
SECTION 02 • SCHEMATIC DESIGN

Circuit Design & ERC Validation

  • Silicon Selection: Centred around a UCC27211 half-bridge gate driver switching two IRFZ44N power MOSFETs.
  • Custom Footprints: Created custom schematic symbols and footprints directly from manufacturer datasheets rather than generic library parts — ensuring every pin assignment matches the physical component exactly.
  • ERC Clearance: The full schematic passed KiCad's Electrical Rules Checker with zero violations before layout began.
SECTION 03 • THERMAL MANAGEMENT

Thermal Design Optimisation

  • Via Matrix Array: A 12-via thermal array beneath the MOSFETs, paired with a copper heatspreader and an inner ground plane, conducts heat away from the switching components.
  • Junction Cooling: Calculated against the MOSFET datasheet, this brought the junction temperature to roughly 31°C under load — well within its 175°C rated maximum.
  • Resistance Cut: Thermal resistance to the heatsink was cut by more than half compared to the original baseline 2-layer board.
SECTION 04 • SIGNAL INTEGRITY

Signal Integrity & Stackup

  • Trace Routing: Gate traces were shortened from 45mm to 12mm and routed alongside the new inner ground plane, reducing trace inductance and pushing the gate ringing frequency from around 20MHz to 54MHz — eliminating a shoot-through risk.
  • Decoupling Optimisation: Tighter decoupling placement cut a voltage spike on the driver's supply pin from 9V to under 0.5V, safely below the driver's switching threshold.
  • 4-Layer Return Path: Added a dedicated ground plane and power plane. This gave the design a continuous return-current path and a heat-spreading layer that the original 2-layer board couldn't provide.
