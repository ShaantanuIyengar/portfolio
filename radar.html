180-Degree Ultrasonic Radar System

Physical hardware-software scan setup calibrated to detect and display target distances on a local LCD screen.

1. Project Overview & Background

Developing mechatronic systems requires a solid understanding of sensor-microcontroller interfacing and timing. This project was engineered to create a functional 180-degree ultrasonic sweep radar. The system scans the surrounding field and calculates distances to log real-time target coordinates.

2. Hardware Interfacing & Servo Integration

The physical build consists of a core microcontroller, an ultrasonic transducer sensor, and a micro servo actuator. I developed firmware to coordinate sweep actions:

  • Hardware Interfacing: Programmed the pulse-width modulation (PWM) control pins of the Arduino to rotate the micro-servo motor in precise increments from 0° to 180°.
  • Range Telemetry: Triggered periodic 10μs sound bursts from the ultrasonic sensor. The echo return timing was processed in firmware to calculate target distances.
  • LCD Screen Output: Integrated a liquid crystal display (LCD) to print the active angle and calculated range parameters on a local hardware screen during operations.

3. Sensor Calibration & Signal Filtering

During initial testing, signal reflections, dispersion, and environmental noise introduced measurement offsets of up to ±10 cm.

To resolve these deviations, I calibrated the sensor offset within the controller firmware. Adjusting for hardware alignment delays and physical limitations improved the precision from ±10 cm to ±6 cm, correcting measurement errors caused by environmental factors and surface reflections.

4. Outcomes & Takeaways

The finished ultrasonic radar scans and displays target coordinates reliably. Through this project, I strengthened my skills in mechatronics prototyping, hardware-software integration, and sensor calibration.

PROJECT TELEMETRY
